# Mechanisms (what made long-running ads work, and what each needs)

From the tested direct-response record (Caples, Sackheim, Hopkins, Schwartz, Halbert, Boardroom, Conroy). Borrow the logic, never the wording, and never the claim. Each one needs real material; if the business does not have it, the mechanism is not available.

| Mechanism | The logic | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| The object | Curiosity forms around one concrete thing ("these mistakes", a 10-day test, a particular bill) | A real, slightly odd object |
| The gap between two things | A familiar want set beside one unfamiliar detail ("300 magazines in 30 minutes") | A true detail that does not seem to belong |
| Contrast | Expected against actual, before against after, two alike people who part ways | Two true states |
| The test | The reader is invited to try something small and see for herself | A step she can do in under a minute |
| Mechanism before product | Explain how it works before naming what is sold | A real process most people have not seen |
| Story with a turn | A scene, an expectation, a reversal | A true story, or one plainly fictional |
| Diagnostic | "Which of these are you doing?" | A real list she will recognize |
| Permission | "Bills it's OK to pay late": lets her do what she wanted to anyway | A true, mildly surprising allowance |
| Named reader | "To people who want to..." | A narrow, real situation, not a trait |
| The offer as the hook | The trial or the free thing is the headline | Real, exact terms |
| Useful on its own | The ad is worth reading even if she never clicks | Real information |

Angle lenses to force spread (use each once): contradiction, hidden cost, the thing everyone else does, a ritual she already has, an unexpected cause, a misdiagnosis, a before and after moment, a comparison, a confession (true or plainly fictional), a demonstration.
