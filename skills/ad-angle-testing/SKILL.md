# Ad angle testing

Source: Adam Taylor's "The death of ad volume" (his agency's reported practice; his figures are his own). Core idea: **more ads is the wrong lever. Every ad is built to prove or kill one idea, and is tagged so the result says what to make next.**

**House rules win.** Read `ads/house-rules.md` first if it exists (claims, testimonials, personal-attribute wording, presenters). Customer phrases are a source of angles; never present an invented story or review as real.

## Terms

- **Angle:** the one idea the whole ad is built around: a specific frustration or desire that makes the right person feel it was made for them. Not the hook, not the format.
- **Awareness level:** what the viewer already knows (table below).

## Plan a batch as a grid, not a list

Bad: 40 ads saying 40 things. Good: **8 angles x 5 variations**. Twelve ads built on three angles are three ads.

Every ad carries these tags before it is made: **angle, persona, awareness level, format, hook, and what it is built to learn.** If the last one is blank, do not make it.

## Where angles come from (customer in, not product out)

| Source | What to take |
|---|---|
| Customer reviews | Exact words for the problem, the fixes that failed, the outcome wanted |
| Reddit and forums | Search the pain, not the brand; copy exact phrasing |
| Comments on competitor ads | What stopped people buying |
| Own account, last 90 days by spend | Why the top three worked |

On a new account with no reviews or history, angles from research are hypotheses: label them so, and say when no customer language was available.

**Long-story native ads without a fake customer:** write in the brand's own voice or about a plainly fictional character. A first-person "customer" story that did not happen is an invented testimonial.

## Match the opening to the awareness level

| Level | How to open |
|---|---|
| Unaware | A symptom they think is normal |
| Problem aware | Name the problem in their words |
| Solution aware | Name what they tried that failed |
| Product aware | Answer the objection that stops them |
| Most aware | Lead with the offer |

Typical accounts put 80 to 90% of ads on the last two levels and run out of people. Tag every live ad by level and fill the gap at the top.

## Creative does the targeting

Brief the **first frame** (setting, framing, place), not only the hook line: a bedroom at night stops a different person than a white background. The landing page must tell the same story as the ad. Optimize for the real goal event (purchase) once there is enough of it to learn from; say so when there is not.

## Read a test: one angle, three formats

| Result | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|
| All three fail | The angle is wrong | Drop it; you know why |
| One format works | The angle works; format was the variable | Put budget on that format |
| All work | Proven angle | Run five openings on it |

## Iterate a winner (the work starts here)

1. Same angle, new format. 2. Five openings per format (different first three seconds). 3. Same angle, opened one awareness level higher. 4. Winning hook on a body that already worked. 5. Scale, and write down why it won.
His example: one hook of five took $30,000 while the other four took under $1,000 together.

## Decide before launch

Write down first: the spend that triggers a review, the cost per result that triggers a stop, and the soft signals (hook rate, hold, clicks) that say an ad has promise before it converts. Give an ad three to five days if it is getting spend. **Spend is the signal**: do not switch off the top spender for being slightly under target.

## When better creative is not the fix

A weak offer, a landing page that tells a different story, the wrong optimization event, or needing results this week.

## Audit an existing account

Last 90 days by spend -> why the top three worked (hook, angle, persona, format) -> awareness level on every live ad -> count distinct angles -> check the optimization event.
