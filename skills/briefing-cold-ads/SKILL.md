# Briefing cold ads

A stranger does not buy the product from an ad. The ad has to make four smaller sales in order, and each one is made with reasons she already half believes. Fill the brief first; writing is then filling in blanks. Source: one practitioner's article (Derick Carneiro), his own reported results, not checked. This skill decides WHAT gets said. `writing-ad-copy` decides how it is written.

**House rules win.** Read `ads/house-rules.md` first if it exists. See "What we do not take" below.

## The brief (fill every row, cite where each answer came from)

| Sale | Element | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Content** (she keeps watching) | Format | What does it look like so she does not feel sold to? |
| | Topic | What subject does she already care about this week? |
| | Concept | The idea in one line: who, doing what, about which topic? |
| | Visual hook | What does she see that breaks autopilot? |
| | Copy hook | The one opening line. |
| **2. Hope** (this could work for me) | Identification | What makes her feel understood? |
| | Point of contact | What does she already suspect is the cause? |
| | Problem mechanism | The missing reason that proves her suspicion right. |
| | Function mechanism | What has to happen instead, and why what she tried cannot do it. |
| | Solution mechanism | The one thing that does it, and how she can see that it does. |
| | Conditions | Why she cannot simply get that on her own. |
| **3. Value** (this product is the place) | Selling proposition | How the product meets each condition, one by one. |
| | Features | Only those that raise the odds, make the wished-for scene concrete, cut effort or cut time. Anything else stays out. |
| | Anchoring | What leaving the problem unsolved costs, next to the price. |
| **4. Impulse** (now) | Risk reversal | Why trying is the safer choice. |
| | Gain now | What she gets by acting today. |
| | Lose later | What she loses by waiting. |

A row with no true answer is left empty and named as empty. An empty row is a finding for the owner, not a gap to write around.

## Order of work

1. **Premises.** Facts already known: product, parts, price, free terms, guarantee. From `ads/product.md`.
2. **Target.** Market (one shared wish), then one segment (a shared situation inside it), then the person. One ad, one segment. Write down two more things, because they decide how much explaining the ad must do:
   - **How aware she is:** of nothing / of her problem / that solutions exist / of this product / ready to act. The less aware, the more the ad must start with her situation and the later the product appears.
   - **How tired the market is:** if she has heard the same promise from many others, a bigger promise will not work. She needs a mechanism: a concrete, checkable reason this works differently.
3. **Brief.** The table above.
4. **Writing.** Take a proven ad as the reference for ORDER and RHYTHM only. The brief supplies every claim.

## Where the answers come from

- Take the point of contact and the mechanisms from what she already believes, not from what the business wants to teach her. Connect dots she has; do not add new ones.
- Research her behavior (what she searches, watches, sends to friends this month) harder than her feelings. Topics, formats and hooks come from there.
- Ads that have run a long time in the same market are finished research. Read them for pains, wording and structure.

## Bets

Count what is new in each ad: segment, mechanism, topic, format, concept, hook. Reuse what has worked for the low-leverage parts (market, segment, mechanism) and place new bets on concept, format and hook. Tag every ad with its bets so a result says which bet paid. A batch is mostly one-bet ads, with a few bolder ones.

## Where the four sales sit when the page must be short

The ad or video carries Content and Hope. The landing page carries Value and Impulse on one screen. If the ad skips Hope, a short page cannot repair it.

## What we do not take from the source

- Inflating fear, or a hook that threatens a loss the product cannot be shown to prevent.
- Scarcity, deadlines or stock limits that are not true. No true reason to act now means the Impulse rows stay empty.
- "The only solution" and other claims the business cannot back.
- "Say what she wants to hear" stops at what is true. A mechanism must be something she can watch happen or check.
- Sexualised hooks, and anything the house rules ban.

## Common mistakes

- Hook, then product: the Hope sale is missing, so the product has no reason to exist yet.
- Listing features nobody asked for ("built on our v3 engine") without saying what they do for her.
- Writing before the brief, then asking a model to guess the psychology.
- Every ad in a batch new in every part: nothing can be read from the result.
